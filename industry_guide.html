💡 The Core Idea — One Sentence

Trenchless technology = installing or repairing underground pipes and cables WITHOUT digging a long open trench.

You only dig a small pit at each end, then push or drill the pipe through underground — the road, building, or river above stays untouched.

Why does this exist?

Imagine a city needs to lay a new water pipe under a 6-lane highway. The old way: close the road for months, dig it up, lay the pipe, fill it in, repave. Cost: enormous. Disruption: massive. Trenchless technology lets you put that pipe under the road in days, with the highway staying open the entire time.

The 3 Core Methods
How pipes actually get installed underground — without digging
🔩

Method 1: Microtunneling / Pipe Jacking

⭐ CP&P's world — this is the most important method to understand

A remote-controlled boring machine (MTBM) is pushed through the ground from a launch pit. Behind it, concrete pipes are pushed in one by one using hydraulic jacks. When the machine reaches the far end, you have a complete underground pipe tunnel — no worker ever goes inside.

How it works — step by step

  1. 1Dig a launch pit and a reception pit at each end of the crossing
  2. 2Lower the MTBM (Microtunneling Boring Machine) into the launch pit
  3. 3The MTBM bores through soil — a cutting head spins and breaks the earth, soil is removed by water/slurry pumped to the surface
  4. 4A concrete jacking pipe is placed behind the MTBM in the pit
  5. 5Hydraulic jacks push the entire string (MTBM + all pipes) forward
  6. 6Another pipe is added, push again — repeat until the MTBM exits at the reception pit
  7. 7Remove the MTBM — a complete pipe tunnel remains underground

Key facts

Pipe Used
Concrete jacking pipe (CP&P makes this) — DN 300–2500mm
Drive Length
Typical 50–500m per drive (sometimes more with intermediate jacking stations)
Used For
Water mains, sewers, storm drains — crossing under roads, railways, rivers
Why Concrete?
The pipe must withstand enormous push force from the jacks — concrete handles compressive load better than plastic
Bentonite connection: During jacking, bentonite mud is pumped around the pipe as lubrication to reduce friction. That's why Siam Industrial Minerals (Booth 9) is relevant — they supply bentonite to contractors who install CP&P pipes.
🔄

Method 2: Horizontal Directional Drilling (HDD)

Flexible pipes — different customers than CP&P but many exhibitors here

A drill rig on the surface pushes a steerable drill head underground at an angle. Electronics track the drill head's exact position. When it exits on the other side, a flexible plastic pipe (HDPE) is attached and pulled back through the drilled path.

How it works

  1. 1HDD rig is set up on one side of the crossing
  2. 2A pilot bore is drilled — the drill head steers underground using electronics
  3. 3Bentonite mud is pumped through the drill to cool it and bring cuttings back
  4. 4After the pilot bore exits, a reamer enlarges the hole to pipe diameter
  5. 5HDPE pipe is attached and pulled back through the enlarged hole
Key difference from microtunneling: HDD pulls a flexible pipe back. Pipe jacking pushes a rigid concrete pipe forward. This is why different pipe types are used.

Method 3: CIPP — Cured-In-Place Pipe Lining

Rehabilitation — fixing old pipes, not installing new ones

CIPP takes an existing old, cracked, or corroded pipe underground and installs a brand-new lining inside it — without any excavation. Think of it like inserting a new pipe inside the old one. After curing, the old pipe has a smooth structural new wall from the inside.

How it works

  1. 1CCTV camera inspects the old pipe to map cracks and measure diameter
  2. 2A felt tube (like a thick sock) soaked in liquid resin is inserted into the pipe — inverted inside-out by water pressure or pulled in
  3. 3The tube is inflated by water/air pressure, pressing against the old pipe wall
  4. 4UV light (or hot water) is passed through the tube — it cures (hardens) the resin into a rigid fiberglass-reinforced pipe
  5. 5Result: old pipe has a brand new structural liner inside, sealed cracks, smooth bore

Why this matters for CP&P

CIPP companies are not direct competitors to CP&P — they fix old pipes, not install new ones. But the same cities that rehab old sewer pipes also build new trunk sewers and water mains — where CP&P's jacking pipes are used. High CIPP activity = infrastructure investment = good market for CP&P too.

Industry Vocabulary — Quick Reference
Terms you'll hear repeatedly at the exhibition

🔩 Pipe & Installation Terms

Jacking Pipe
A concrete pipe specifically designed to be pushed ("jacked") through the ground under enormous hydraulic force. Much stronger than ordinary concrete pipe.
Pipe Jacking
The method of installing pipes by pushing them through soil using hydraulic jacks in a launch pit. Pipes are pushed one at a time until the string spans the entire crossing.
Microtunneling
Pipe jacking where the boring machine is remote-controlled — no worker inside the tunnel. Smaller diameters where a person cannot fit (typically DN 300–1500mm).
MTBM
Microtunneling Boring Machine — the remote-controlled machine that bores through soil ahead of the jacking pipe string. Has a cutting head that spins and breaks the earth.
TBM
Tunnel Boring Machine — a larger version of the MTBM, used for metro tunnels, road tunnels, and large water tunnels. Workers operate inside. Herrenknecht makes both.
DN
Diameter Nominal — the standard pipe size designation. DN 300 = 300mm internal diameter. DN 2500 = 2.5 metre. CP&P makes DN 300 to DN 2500.
Launch Pit
The starting excavation where the MTBM and jacking equipment are installed. This is the only significant digging needed for the entire pipe crossing.
Reception Pit
The far-end excavation where the MTBM exits after completing the drive. The machine is retrieved here and lifted out.
Intermediate Jacking Station (IJS)
A hydraulic jack unit installed mid-pipe-string to allow longer drives. Used when a single launch-pit jack set cannot push the entire string the full distance.
Jacking Force
The hydraulic push force applied to the pipe string, measured in tonnes or kN. Longer drives and harder soils require higher jacking force — concrete handles this better than GRP or plastic.

🔄 HDD Terms

HDD
Horizontal Directional Drilling — a trenchless method that steers a drill head underground and pulls a flexible pipe back through the drilled path. Uses HDPE pipe, not concrete.
Pilot Bore
The first, small-diameter hole drilled along the planned path in HDD. After this is complete, it's enlarged with a reamer before pulling the pipe through.
Reamer
The cutting tool pulled back through the pilot bore to enlarge it to the diameter needed for the product pipe. Multiple passes may be needed for large diameters.
Walkover Guidance
A ground-level tracking system where an operator walks above the drill path and reads the signal from a sonde (transmitter) in the drill head to track its position.

🩹 Pipe Materials & Rehabilitation

GRP / FRP
Glass Reinforced Plastic / Fibre Reinforced Plastic — fiberglass pipe. Strong, light, corrosion-resistant. Competes with concrete jacking pipe. Amiblu is the world's largest GRP pipe maker.
HDPE / PE
High-Density Polyethylene — black plastic pipe. Flexible, corrosion-immune, lightweight. Used in HDD. Competes with concrete/GRP for smaller diameter gravity sewers.
PE Lining
CP&P's key differentiator — a polyethylene layer bonded to the inside of the concrete pipe. Protects the concrete from corrosive wastewater, solving concrete's main weakness.
CIPP
Cured-In-Place Pipe — a rehabilitation method. A resin-soaked felt liner is inserted into an old pipe and cured (hardened) in place, forming a new pipe inside the old one. No excavation needed.
SPR (Spiral Pipe Renewal)
A rehabilitation method where a PVC or steel-reinforced profile is spirally wound inside an existing pipe, forming a new structural lining. Iseki and Sekisui both use this technology.
UV Curing
Using ultraviolet light to harden resin-soaked CIPP liners. UV curing is faster and more controlled than older thermal (hot water) curing methods.
Bentonite
A natural clay mineral used as drilling fluid in HDD and microtunneling. Mixed with water, it lubricates the boring, removes cuttings, and stabilizes the hole. Siam Industrial Minerals (Booth 9) supplies this.
Slurry
In microtunneling, a liquid mixture (usually water + bentonite) that carries excavated soil from the cutting head back to the surface through pipes. The MTBM uses slurry to remove cuttings.

📡 Inspection & Locating

CCTV Inspection
Closed-circuit TV inspection — a camera is pushed or driven through a pipe to inspect its condition. Utilities do this after installation (QA) and periodically during service life.
Crawler Camera
A wheeled robot that carries a camera through the pipe. Controlled by a technician above ground via cable. Used for larger diameter pipes (DN 150+).
GPR
Ground-Penetrating Radar — sends radar pulses into the ground to detect buried pipes, cables, and voids from the surface. Used before any excavation to map what's underground.
Sonde
A radio transmitter placed in a drill head or pipe camera. Radiodetection/DCI receivers above ground pick up the signal to track the exact position of the device underground.
